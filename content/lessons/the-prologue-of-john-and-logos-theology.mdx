# John 1:1–18: The Prologue and Logos Christology

> *Note: This content is preserved from the original website prototype and is designated as curriculum study material.*

## Expository Outline

The prologue to the Gospel of John represents one of the most profound theological syntheses in ancient literature, inaugurating the narrative with the cosmic pre-existence and incarnation of the eternal Word.

### Theological Foundations

1. **The Pre-Existence and Deity of the Logos (John 1:1–5)**:
   - "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God."
   - The distinction of persons within the divine Godhead (*pros ton theon*) alongside unity of essence (*theos en ho logos*).
   - The agency of Christ in creation: all things were made through Him.

2. **The Witness of John the Baptist (John 1:6–9)**:
   - The transitional role of the forerunner, bearing witness to the True Light coming into the darkness of the world.

3. **Rejection and Adoption (John 1:10–13)**:
   - He came to His own, and His own people did not receive Him.
   - But to all who did receive Him, He gave the right to become children of God, born not of blood nor the will of man, but of God.

4. **The Incarnation of Glory (John 1:14–18)**:
   - "And the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen His glory, glory as of the only Son from the Father, full of grace and truth."
   - Moses brought the Law; grace and truth came through Jesus Christ.
