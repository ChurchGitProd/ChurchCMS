# Romans 5–8: Grace, Justification, and Life in the Spirit

> *Note: This content is preserved from the original website prototype and is designated as curriculum study material.*

## Expository Outline

Romans 5–8 forms the theological climax of Paul's soteriological discourse in his Epistle to the Romans, moving from peace with God to eternal security in Christ Jesus.

### Core Sections

1. **Peace and Hope Through Justification (Romans 5:1–11)**:
   - Having been justified by faith, believers have objective peace with God and rejoice in the hope of the glory of God.
   - God demonstrates His love toward us in that while we were still sinners, Christ died for us.

2. **The Two Adams: Adam and Christ (Romans 5:12–21)**:
   - Federal headship explained: just as through one man sin entered the world and death through sin, so much more through the one Man Jesus Christ grace reigns through righteousness.

3. **Dead to Sin, Alive in Christ (Romans 6:1–23)**:
   - The doctrine of vital union with Christ: buried with Him by baptism into death, in order that just as Christ was raised, we too might walk in newness of life.
   - Slaves of sin vs. slaves of righteousness yielding the fruit of holiness.

4. **The Law and the Inner Conflict (Romans 7:1–25)**:
   - The believer is released from the condemnation of the Law to serve in the new way of the Spirit.

5. **No Condemnation and Life in the Spirit (Romans 8:1–39)**:
   - The law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus has set you free from the law of sin and death.
   - The adoption as sons, the intercession of the Holy Spirit, and the unbreakable golden chain of salvation (Romans 8:28–30).
