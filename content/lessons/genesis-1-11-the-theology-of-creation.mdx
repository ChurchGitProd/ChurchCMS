# Genesis 1–11: The Theology of Creation and Covenant

> *Note: This content is preserved from the original website prototype and is designated as curriculum study material.*

## Overview & Historical Setting

The primeval history recorded in Genesis 1–11 serves as the foundational worldview architecture for the entire biblical canon. In this expository study, we explore the literary symmetry of the creation week, the cosmic temple paradigm, and the initial promises of redemption following the Fall.

### Key Exegetical Themes

1. **The Sovereign Creator (Genesis 1:1–2:3)**:
   - Creation *ex nihilo* and the ordering of cosmic realms into habitable spaces.
   - The functional and ontological significance of mankind created in the *imago Dei*.

2. **The Covenant of Works and Fall (Genesis 2:15–3:24)**:
   - The probation of Adam in Eden as priest-king.
   - The entrance of sin and the *Protoevangelium* (Genesis 3:15) as the inaugural announcement of the seed of the woman.

3. **Covenant Continuity Through Judgment (Genesis 6–9)**:
   - The universal flood narrative demonstrating divine holiness and covenant preservation through the Noahic ark.

4. **The Nations and the Call of Grace (Genesis 10–11)**:
   - The rebellion at Babel juxtaposed against God's sovereign plan to bless all nations through the line of Shem and ultimately Abraham.
