## Lecture Overview: The Abrahamic Promise Explored

In this theological lecture, Dr. Charles Vance explores the covenant ratification ritual recorded in Genesis 15. Grounded in the legal forms of second-millennium BC Ancient Near Eastern suzerainty treaties, this passage reveals the unconditional guarantee underpinning God's promises to Abraham.

### The Covenant Ceremony Analysis

#### 1. The Cutting of the Animals (*Berith*)
In biblical idiom, covenants are literally "cut" (*karath berith*). Sacrificial animals were divided in two, creating a pathway between the halves. In standard legal practice, both contracting parties would walk between the severed pieces, swearing a conditional self-maledictory oath: *"May it be done to me as has been done to these animals if I fail to uphold the terms of this covenant."*

#### 2. The Unilateral Divine Guarantee
In Genesis 15:12–17, a deep sleep falls upon Abram, leaving him passive. As darkness descends:
- A smoking fire pot and a flaming torch—symbols of the divine Shekinah glory—pass between the animal pieces alone.
- God assumes unilateral liability for the covenant promises, pledging His own eternal life that the seed and the land will be granted to Abraham's offspring.

### Theological Application in the New Testament
Paul builds directly upon this foundational reality in Galatians 3:17–18 and Romans 4, showing that salvation by grace through faith is anchored in this sovereign, irrevocable oath made centuries before the Sinai Law.
