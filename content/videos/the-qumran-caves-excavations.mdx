## Lecture Overview: The Qumran Caves Excavations

Dr. Thomas Keller examines the extraordinary discovery of the Dead Sea Scrolls in eleven caves near Khirbet Qumran on the northwestern shore of the Dead Sea between 1947 and 1956. This archaeological milestone bridged a thousand-year gap in Hebrew manuscript history.

### Historical Context & Manuscript Discoveries

#### 1. The Great Isaiah Scroll (*1QIsaᵃ*)

Discovered in Cave 1, the Great Isaiah Scroll contains the complete Hebrew text of the book of Isaiah, dated paleographically and via radiocarbon analysis to approximately 125 BC. When compared with the Masoretic text copied over a millennium later in the Leningrad Codex (1008 AD), scholars found astonishing word-for-word textual fidelity, confirming the reliability of biblical manuscript transmission.

#### 2. The Community of Qumran

The manuscripts reflect an ascetic Jewish community—widely associated with the Essenes—who retreated to the Judean desert to live in strict ritual purity and devoted themselves to the perpetual copying, preservation, and study of sacred Scripture.

### Key Archaeological Takeaways

* **Textual Witnesses**: The presence of Proto-Masoretic, Pre-Samaritan, and Septuagintal text-types demonstrates the vibrant textual history of late Second Temple Judaism.
* **Messianic Expectations**: The sectarian scrolls illuminate the intense messianic anticipation that characterized Judea immediately preceding the advent of Christ.
