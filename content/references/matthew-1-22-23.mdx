## Scripture Focus: Matthew 1:22-23

> "All this took place to fulfill what the Lord had spoken by the prophet: 'Behold, the virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and they shall call his name Immanuel' (which means, God with us)."

### Prophetic Foundation & Linguistic Analysis

In Matthew's infancy narrative, the evangelist identifies the birth of Jesus as the ultimate realization of the oracle delivered by Isaiah to King Ahaz of Judah during the Syro-Ephraimite crisis (circa 734 BC).

#### 1. Hebrew *'Almah* vs. Greek *Parthenos*
In the original Masoretic text of Isaiah 7:14, the Hebrew term used is **עַלְמָה** (*'almah*), denoting a young maiden of marriageable age who is assumed to be chaste. When the Jewish scholars in Alexandria translated the Hebrew Scriptures into the Greek Septuagint (LXX) in the 3rd to 2nd centuries BC, they rendered *'almah* with the more specific Greek term **παρθένος** (*parthenos*), which unambiguously denotes a virgin. 

Matthew under divine inspiration adopts the Septuagint's explicit formulation, showing that what had a proximate, typological significance in Ahaz's era found its definitive, supernatural fulfillment in Jesus of Nazareth.

### Theological Implications

1. **Divine Initiative**: The incarnation is fundamentally an act of God entering human history from beyond the natural order.
2. **Covenant Continuity**: Matthew does not present Christianity as an abrupt rupture, but as the blossoming of promises spoken centuries prior.
3. **The Immanuel Principle**: The designation *Immanuel* underscores that Jesus is not merely an anointed messenger, but God Himself tabernacling among His people.
