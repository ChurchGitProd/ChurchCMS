## Scripture Focus: Psalm 22:1-31

> "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me? Why are you so far from saving me, from the words of my groaning?" (Psalm 22:1)

### Literary Architecture & Typological Parallels

Psalm 22 represents the apex of Davidic lament literature, transitioning midway (verse 22) from profound anguish to cosmic praise. While rooted in King David's personal experience of acute suffering, the psalm transcends historical Davidic circumstances, depicting sensory and physical horrors that foreshadow the Roman execution of the Messiah.

### The Movement from Agony to Global Vindication

The latter half of Psalm 22 (vv. 22–31) broadens its horizon from a solitary sufferer to a universal assembly:
- *"All the ends of the earth shall remember and turn to the Lord."* (v. 27)
- *"Posterity shall serve him; it shall be told of the Lord to the coming generation."* (v. 30)

This demonstrates that Christ's citation on the cross was not a despairing cry of theological defeat, but an invocation of the entire psalm—pointing all witness to its ultimate triumphant conclusion.
